# GRIDTECH chronological model rules v5.5.1
## 1. Clock, ramp and customer timing
Use all model_hour values 0..87599, each one hour. Development month = floor(model_hour / 730), model year = floor(model_hour / 8760) + 1, reference_hour = model_hour mod 8760. Month 0 is hypothetical development start; month 18 starts at hour 13140 (grid milestone, not default opening); month 20 starts at hour 14600. Months are equal 730-hour screening periods, not Gregorian months. Reference timestamps describe the 2023 observation only and must not be interpreted as future timestamps.

The default customer begins service at month 20 and reaches full demand at month 30. An illustrative accelerated request begins service at month 14 and reaches full demand at month 24. Earliest physical readiness is month 14. The customer target for full demand is month 30; explain whether your supported customer input treats it as hard or preferred. An alternative initial_service_month must be an integer >= 14 with initial_service_month + 10 < 120 (maximum initial service month 109). All comparisons end at month 120; there is no free demand suppression after the chosen start.

Before initial service: demand = 0. At initial service: load_fraction = 1/3. Each subsequent model month adds 1/15 to the fraction until 1 at month start + 10; thereafter it stays 1. Thus for relative month k >= 0, fraction = min(1, 1/3 + k/15). Apply the fraction to each mature hourly tier. commissioning_ramp.csv provides the exact common scale factors. The baseline timeline is already expanded in timeline_default.csv. Relocatable/interruptible shares still vary by reference hour. The 10 MW mature essential-infrastructure floor scales with occupancy; it is not an extra 10 MW applied before opening.

Changing start dates shifts the commissioning ramp only. Grid and energy-resource availability dates stay anchored to development start, and so do the price and grid-CO2 trajectories (§5, data/price_trajectory.json). Initial power and full-campus power are separate milestones. You need not optimize the ramp shape or installation dates. State the dates your search actually tested; a failed search is not proof of impossibility.

## 2. Grid and energy resources
Required base access: 0 MW before month 18, 25 MW from month 18, 50 MW from month 24, 75 MW from month 30. Each upgrade takes effect during the modeled timeline. Optional grid_delay_months shifts every positive milestone by that nonnegative integer; zero is the required base case. Resource dates do not move. Any delay length is permitted; show which values you tested.

Select capacities in catalog blocks and below maxima. For each selected energy resource, operational availability and common commitment charges begin at max(initial_service_month, resource.available_month), and capacity remains committed through month 120. For example, selecting nuclear for a month 20 start gives zero output and no nuclear charge before month 60, then available-generation payments from month 60 even if some available energy is curtailed. No nuclear selection is required. There are no hidden construction or retirement choices.

All dispatched solar/wind/nuclear contract electricity plus merchant purchases share the grid-import cap. No export/resale. Contracts do not expand the connection or provide islanded backup. Wind/solar dispatch <= selected MW * hourly profile * availability mask. Nuclear uses its supplied flat contractual ceiling, not an outage/reliability guarantee. Gas resources use constant heat rates; fuel infrastructure is assumed ready at their dates.

Sum of selected onsite MW * land_acres_per_mw <= 8 acres. Reserve the full selected equipment envelope, including later resources. This is a common conservative reservation rule, not optimized site layout. Offsite resources occupy no campus acreage.

## 3. Tier response and hourly balance
Requested facility AC demand includes allocated cooling. No additional PUE multiplier. Every hour equals Tier 0 + Tier 1 + Tier 2. Tier 0 must remain local and served. During active stress intervals only, Tier 1 may relocate within both its demand and remote electricity capacity / 1.05. Tier 2 may be interrupted within its demand * allowed fraction. Outside events both actions are zero. Tier 1 is served elsewhere with no local rebound; Tier 2 is canceled with a consequence charge, not deferred.

Conservation-window timings come from public ERCOT notices. The 60% campus import allowance, guaranteed 30-minute notice and service consequences are fictional exercise assumptions. Event patterns repeat once per model year; these are not predictions or actual compulsory campus orders. Notice is available at least 30 minutes before each affected interval, so full-hour relocation is allowed. No subhourly response simulation is required.

Hourly: dispatched generation + merchant purchases + battery discharge = load − Tier 1 relocation − Tier 2 interruption + battery charge. Every flow is nonnegative. No unreported unmet load. Excess available generation can be curtailed; generation curtailment is distinct from customer interruption. Effective grid cap = ordinary cap * event fraction (1 outside events). Onsite generation can reduce grid imports without interrupting demand.

Remote capacity = 12 MW electricity, or 11.428571 MW local-equivalent relocation at 1.05 remote MWh per local MWh. Assume the receiving site is available during local events; no remote network or queuing model. Relocation consequence = 1.05 * 70 + 50 = 123.50 USD per local MWh; interruption consequence = 1000 USD/MWh. These are fictional common valuations, not measured revenue losses.

## 4. Storage
Battery internal usable energy = selected MW * 4 hours. Charge/discharge <= selected MW, only after availability. One-way efficiency = sqrt(0.85). End-hour SOC[t] = SOC[t−1] + eta * charge[t] − discharge[t] / eta; SOC before hour 0 = 0 and SOC at the final hour = 0. It remains within [0, 4 * MW] and carries continuously across month/year boundaries. No simultaneous charge/discharge and no midyear/annual energy reset.

Annual equivalent internal discharge = sum(discharge / eta) / (4 * MW) <= 365 * available_hours_in_model_year / 8760. For zero battery, all three battery columns are zero. Charging <= contemporaneous dispatched clean portfolio generation. Merchant and fossil charging are not allowed under this exercise convention. Ignore degradation and replacement; all catalog lives exceed the horizon.

## 5. Common costs and commitments
All dollars are constant 2023 USD. Merchant wholesale energy, the Henry Hub component of delivered gas, and grid CO2 are not flat across the horizon: for model_hour h each is multiplied by (1 + r)^(h/8760), using the real annual rates r in data/price_trajectory.json (currently wholesale +5%, Henry Hub +3%, grid CO2 −3% per year), equal to 1.0 at model_hour 0. The 20 USD/MWh merchant adder, 1 USD/MMBtu gas adder, contract prices, equipment costs and response prices stay fixed. `timeline_default.csv` and `prepare_inputs()` already include this path; if you rebuild inputs by hand from `reference_hourly.csv` you must apply it too. Annual equipment charge = MW * 1000 * (CAPEX_per_kW * CRF + fixed O&M_per_kW_year), with CRF = r / [1 − (1 + r)^(−life)], r = 0.08. Charge 1/8760 of this amount per available hour after commitment start. Retain it even if later supply displaces dispatch. Do not also charge upfront CAPEX or add a resale/terminal credit. This is a sum of annualized screening costs, NOT project cash-flow NPV; no second discounting is applied to annualized charges.

Available-generation contracts pay price * selected MW * profile for ALL available hours after commitment start, whether dispatched or curtailed. Do not also charge catalog equipment CAPEX for these offers or merchant prices for contracted energy. Gas dispatch pays catalog payment_usd_per_mwh (variable O&M) plus HHV heat_rate * delivered gas price. Merchant dispatch pays hourly wholesale price plus service adder, including negative prices. Charging energy is paid through its source, not charged twice.

Keep local energy cost, remote relocation charges and interruption consequences separate; their sum is total_decision_cost_usd. Report horizon totals and model-year results. Local cost/MWh divides by remaining local demand; common decision cost/MWh divides by requested demand before response. Keep dates and requested/served/relocated/interrupted MWh visible: delayed opening supplies less energy within a fixed horizon, so a lower total alone does not establish a better outcome. Customer-specific delay valuations, carbon prices or willingness-to-pay are team-designed and separately labeled.

## 6. Environmental comparison
Local CO2 = sum(merchant MWh * grid factor) + sum(gas MWh * resource factor). Remote CO2 = remote electricity MWh * remote grid factor (the same declining grid-factor path as local merchant electricity). Combined = local + remote. Solar/wind/nuclear have zero operational CO2 in this benchmark, not zero lifecycle impacts. Gas factors = HHV heat_rate * 117 lb CO2/MMBtu * 0.00045359237 t/lb.

Primary combined electricity intensity = 1000 * combined CO2 tonnes / (local served MWh + remote electricity MWh). Use combined_electricity_co2_kg_per_mwh for the illustrative 100 kg threshold; show annual and horizon results, with the horizon ratio calculated from totals, not an average of ratios. Also report interrupted/relocated MWh. A separate service intensity uses requested minus interrupted local-equivalent MWh; it is not the same denominator.

Local clean MWh = dispatched clean generation − battery charge + battery discharge. Divide by remaining local served MWh. This accounts for storage losses and avoids duplicate clean credit. Grid and remote electricity receive zero contracted clean credit even though their physical mix contains some clean generation. Annual clean share is not hourly clean coverage; factors are screening indicators, not marginal avoided emissions or lifecycle inventories.

## 7. Historical and fictional boundaries
The same 2023 aligned reference year repeats 10 times, preserving price/renewable coincidence without inventing future observations. Grid CO2 remains an annual-average factor (not hourly or marginal) that declines smoothly along the trajectory; gas prices keep the repeated 2023 monthly shape; merchant wholesale, Henry Hub gas and grid CO2 follow the real-terms trajectory in data/price_trajectory.json; all other costs are un-escalated. Weather risk, forced outages, gas delivery constraints, startup/ramp constraints and market evolution beyond the supplied smooth trajectory are not required inputs. LZ_SOUTH is a regional price proxy, not a verified Williamson County node. Offers, site limits and dates are fictional and clearly sourced as CASE. Optional extensions must disclose extra assumptions and retain a reproducible common-model result.

## 8. Clarifications (v5.5.1)
- **Land limits the gas turbine to 75 MW.** The catalog maximum is 100 MW, but 100 MW × 0.1 acres/MW = 10 acres exceeds the 8-acre site. Any selection must satisfy both the catalog maximum and the land rule.
- **`maximum_event_duration_hours` (7) is descriptive.** It equals the longest supplied event; no separate duration limit applies beyond the supplied event windows.
- **Building inputs for other opening months.** `prepare_inputs()` in `starter/check_submission.py` expands the reference year using these rules and may be imported by teams (see README §2). It builds inputs only; it does not dispatch or choose a portfolio.
